# Content

A prototype is judged on its words as much as on its pixels. Generic content hides generic thinking.

- **Real data.** Names, amounts, dates and titles that could exist in this product. No "Lorem ipsum", no "John Doe", no "Item 1". Dates agree with each other, totals add up.
- **Uneven data.** Real lists have a long name, an empty field, an old date. Include them on purpose.
- **Say what it does.** "Send invoice", not "Submit". "Delete loan", not "Confirm". The button tells what happens after the click.
- **Write for the person, not the system.** "We could not reach your bank", not "Error 503: upstream timeout".
- **One name per thing.** If it is a "loan" on the first screen, it stays a loan on the last.
- **No filler headline.** "Welcome back" and "Everything you need" say nothing. Use the space for the state of things: "3 items due this week".
- **Short.** Cut the first draft in half. A sentence in the interface does one job.
- **No fake proof.** No invented logos, testimonials or metrics, unless the topic is about them and says they are placeholders.

Write the content before polishing the layout: the longest real value decides the layout, not the other way around.
