# Tells

What makes a screen look generated. Refuse these unless the brief asks for them. Read this file before calling a screen done.

- A centered hero with a big title, a subtitle and two buttons, on a screen that is not a landing page.
- Three cards in a row with an icon, a title and two lines of text.
- A gradient on text, on the page background, or behind a card.
- A colored stripe on the left edge of every card.
- An icon above every heading, chosen because a slot existed.
- Emoji used as icons.
- A pill-shaped badge on everything.
- Every card with the same shadow, radius and padding, whatever it holds.
- Four metric tiles on top of a dashboard, whether or not those numbers matter.
- Decorative blobs, grids or glows filling empty space.
- A sidebar and a top bar on a product with four pages.
- Round numbers everywhere: 100, 1,000, 50%.
- Headlines made of three adjectives or a "not X, but Y" turn.

A good test: replace the product name with another one. If nothing else has to change, the screen is not about this product yet.

Refusing a tell is not a goal in itself. A brief, a brand or a reference always wins over this list.
